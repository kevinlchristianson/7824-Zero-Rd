Sources & assumptions

  • Plan is scaled from Google Earth. The ruler reads 82.33 ft along the north leg's north roof edge, which is about 0.33 ft per pixel. Overall proportions were checked against the listing's overhead drone shot.
  • The center block and the shop are 48′ × 68′ inside (yours), with about 16″ of brick and foam around them. The buildings butt together; the overlap seen from above is the eaves. The north leg's extra length to the measured roof edge is a vestibule behind the four west arches.
  • The center wall stack is 11′ ground level, then 2′ of floor structure, then 9′ upper level, for a 22′ plate.
  • Upper-level openings are yours: sixteen 41″ × 67″ windows, a 36″ door at the stair and a 72″ door, drawn as a slider onto a small north balcony (placement assumed).
  • The arched porch is 10′ deep and stands outside the 48′ × 68′ box, with the upper-level deck on top (yours). The ground level has no windows: a 36″ walkout onto the porch and 86″ walkthroughs into the shop and south leg. The shop has no windows and no doors on the windward side (yours).
  • Leg wall heights, the 5:12 roof pitch, the 1′‑6″ overhang, exact door positions, and the vestibule and south-leg openings are estimates from the listing photos.
  • All three roofs are gables (yours). The ridges are assumed to run the long way, north–south on the center and east–west on the legs, so only the legs have south slopes.
  • The max fit puts a panel on every spot that pays on the south, east and west slopes, with your plan's clearances, and keeps your rows on the shop. It is priced with the Solar tab's model: its panel, inverter and install costs, your rates, the same heat pump setup, and one EG4 18kPV per 18 kW of panels.
  • Panels are your ZnShine 440 W, 68″ × 45″, ½″ apart, kept 12″ clear of eaves and gable ends and 18″ off ridges. The shop has 72: your IronRidge layout (57 panels) carried east as far as the center block's shade allows. Shade from the other buildings is worked out hour by hour on the Casper weather year; trees are left out. npm run roofpv rebuilds the layout in data/roofpv.json.
  • All inputs live in model/params.js, shared with the heating and cooling model. npm run export writes OBJ and GLB files to export/.
